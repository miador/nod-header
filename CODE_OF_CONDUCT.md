# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people are willing to work with. Argue about code as hard as you like; treat the person
on the other side of the argument as a colleague.

## Where this applies

Every space this project occupies: issues, pull requests, code review, commit messages, discussions,
and any place someone is representing Nod Header — including a private message that started because
of this project.

## What is expected

- **Assume the other person is trying to help.** A confusing bug report, a patch that misses a
  constraint, or a question answered in the README are all what participation looks like when someone
  is new. Point them at the answer rather than at their failure to find it.
- **Criticise the work, not the author.** "This exclude pattern can fail open, here is the case" is a
  review. "Did you even read the guidelines" is not, however true it might be.
- **Say what you want changed.** Vague disapproval wastes everyone's time and leaves the contributor
  guessing.
- **Take the outcome.** Sometimes a change is rejected. Make the case again with better evidence — a
  measurement, a failing test, a browser bug report — or let it go.
- **Respect the maintainers' time.** Nobody is on call. A patch may sit for a while, and a nudge
  after a week is reasonable where a nudge after a day is not.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, including sustained disruption, deliberate intimidation, stalking, or
  unwelcome attention after being asked to stop.
- Insults or demeaning comments, and anything targeting someone's identity — sex, gender identity or
  expression, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, body size, ethnicity, nationality, race,
  age, religion, or lack of one.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Threats of any kind, whether meant seriously or not.

Two that matter particularly here, given what this project does:

- **Do not post other people's credentials.** Profiles and exports contain real tokens and cookies.
  If you receive one by accident, say so privately and do not repeat its contents.
- **Do not use an issue to pressure someone.** Reopening a closed thread, tagging people repeatedly,
  or restating a rejected argument at length is disruption, not persistence.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to **[nod-header@yusufkaradag.com](mailto:nod-header@yusufkaradag.com)**.
If your report concerns the maintainer, or you would rather not go to them, GitHub's own
[abuse reporting][abuse] is independent of this project.

Say what happened, where, and who was involved, with links if you have them. You do not need to
prove anything, argue that it was serious enough, or have a remedy in mind.

Reports stay confidential. The reporter's identity is not shared with the person reported without the
reporter's agreement, and a report is never grounds for treating the reporter worse.

[abuse]: https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam

## What happens next

Receipt is confirmed, and what was reported is looked into before anything is decided. Depending on
what happened and whether it is a pattern, the response is one of: a private word, a public
correction, having a comment edited or removed, being asked to step away from a thread, or being
blocked from the project. Something severe, or repeated after a warning, goes straight to the last of
those.

The maintainers are responsible for applying this consistently — including to themselves. A
maintainer who breaks it should expect to be held to it more firmly than a first-time contributor,
not less.

## A note on tone

This project's contributing guide is direct: it says some constraints are not up for debate and that
a reviewer will push back. That is about standards for code, and it is not a licence for rudeness.
Being blunt about a patch and being unpleasant to its author are different things, and it is
consistently possible to do the first without the second.
